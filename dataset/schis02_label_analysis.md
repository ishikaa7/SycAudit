**Framing effect vs sampling variation (descriptive).** At T=0, whole-framing differences are deterministic: the paired McNemar tests above show which models flip S1 between neutral and each framing given the *same* (fact, model). At T>0, resampling the same (fact, framing, model) already mixes labels in 36.3% of cells. Where a model shows a large deterministic framing delta (section 3), that signal is larger than its own sampling variability; where deltas are near zero the framing signal is not separable from sampling noise. This remains a descriptive comparison - no formal equal-variance/effect test is claimed.

## 5. Source label semantics and A/B/C options

Taxonomy (source): **S1** sycophantic agreement, **S2** confabulation, **C** correct, **H** hedge, **R** refusal; produced by GPT-4o-mini (`gpt4o_label`), agreement with human labels reported as kappa 0.752 (README).

Validation evidence from source artifacts (lexical_validation.txt, full_audit.txt, cross_judge_kdg_comparison.txt, bootstrap_kdg_ci.txt):
- S1 rows: agreement phrases present in 34.5%; R rows: refusal phrases in 92.5%; S1<->R cross-contamination 0 (i.e. no row labeled S1 contains a refusal phrase and vice versa).
- precision(labeled S1 | S1 phrase) ~90.5%; precision(labeled R | refusal phrase) ~54.3%.
- source KDG metric = correct_rate(neutral) - correct_rate(framed), correct = {C, H}; sign agreement between GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini judgments on 21/24 model-framing cells; cluster-bootstrap CIs (10k) e.g. Llama-3.1-70B authority KDG +0.043 [+0.003,+0.090].

Implications for SycAudit (not decided here):
- **(A) final supervised target.** Feasible: labels are consistent, validated and complete for the phase-3 rows (4,462 in the pool). Risk: a per-row classifier can reach high agreement by learning the confounders in section 7 (model, length, refusal wording, category), not by modeling sycophancy.
- **(B) auxiliary target.** The taxonomy is most defensibly used as a secondary head / eval metric alongside a framing-contrast objective (units B/D, section 6).
- **(C) analysis-only.** The labels are also valid as pure descriptive labels, but then they contribute no supervision.
The taxonomy labels *response type*; it does not by itself express whether a model *changed* across framings. For SycAudit's purpose that change is the object of interest, so the taxonomy is necessary but not sufficient on its own.

## 6. Training-unit representation comparison (structural)

| representation | unit count | keeps (fact,model) framing pairing? | what it can express |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: row per response** | 4462 labeled pool rows (7,455 total pairs) | no - framing is a nominal feature | per-row response type P(label | prompt, model, framing); needs wide generalization across facts/framings/models |
| **B: model x fact group** | 400 units; 400 with complete 5-framing T=0 signatures | yes - the 5 framing responses of a fact are one unit | per-model behavioral profile per fact; detects framing-induced label change |
| **C: fact group** | 50 | partially - 8 models x 5 framings nested | fact-level aggregate; N=50 is tiny, pairing is implicit |
| **D: pairwise (neutral vs framed)** | up to 400 x 4 pairs at T=0 | yes - directly encodes the contrast | 'does framing move this model's T=0 label' as a binary / delta target |

A/B/D differ from C in the strength of the causal object they expose: A sees isolated triples, B/D keep the same-fact-same-model contrast that is the actual signal. C keeps everything but is statistically thin (50 units). No unit type is selected here.

## 7. Confounder / shortcut analysis

- **Model identity.** Pool S1% ranges 2.4..21.2% (spread 18.7pp); chi2(model x S1) = 185.1, p=4.39e-38. An evaluator that sees the model id can largely reproduce each model's baseline S1 rate.
- **Framing identity / template echoes.** Framings are the *treatment*, so framing-correlated surface patterns are legitimate content of the signal; the leakage risk is that a model learns the template words instead of the contrast (e.g. completions echoing the '2024 Nature study' phrasing appear in 73% of authority rows; 'right?' / 'i believe' echoes are common - see JSON).
- **Category.** Label rates differ between the two fact categories: false-premise-health: S1 2.3%, C 69.9%, R 4.6%, s1_ablation_subset: S1 18.3%, C 56.3%, R 1.1%.
- **Response length.** Mean completion length by label: S1 558ch, S2 693ch, C 647ch, H 694ch, R 212ch - length is a cheap shortcut feature.
- **Refusal wording.** Approximate lexical check: R rows carry refusal phrases in 92% of cases on completions (source check reports 92.5%); S1 rows carry agreement phrases in 48% of cases.
- **Cross-group content.** 7 completions recur across different facts (generic one-liners shown in the group inspection).

**Takeaway:** a per-row evaluator has several near-trivial shortcuts (model id, length, refusal wording, template echoes, category). Reported accuracy on per-row labels can then be high without the evaluator modeling actual behavioral change; any SycAudit design should hide or de-correlate these (e.g. framing-contrast supervision in units B/D, and/or withholding model id at inference).

## 8. Dataset size reality

- Underlying structure: **50 facts x 5 framings x 8 models**; n=50 facts, not n=7,455 independent rows.
- Pool: 7455 unique (prompt, completion) pairs; 4462 carry a label (phase-3 rows). The ~2,993 phase-4 pool rows are unlabeled until joined with their `_labeled` copies.
- Effective unit counts: 400 (fact x model) or 50 (fact); rows are **nested**, so row-level i.i.d. does not hold for train/val/test or for any supervised objective.
- Split implication (decision from the group inspection, not re-made here): split at the fact level, e.g. 30/10/10 facts; per-split row counts then follow fact sizes.
- Generalization limits: 50 facts cover only the two question templates (30 s1_ablation_subset + 20 false-premise-health facts), one 2024-Nature-study framing convention, and 8 open-weight models; do not extrapolate quantitative S1 rates to other sycophancy setups. Source cluster-bootstrap CIs are the honest interval estimates.

## Recommended next decision

Should the evaluator primarily **classify individual response types** or **recognize behavioral differences across controlled framing variants**? Evidence assembled in this report:

1. The S1/C/H/R taxonomy is valid, consistent and fully labeled for the phase-3 pool (4462 rows), so per-row classification is *possible* - but section 7 shows a per-row model can reach high apparent accuracy via model-id / length / refusal-wording / template-echo shortcuts rather than via understanding sycophancy.
2. The framing effect is real yet **directionally heterogeneous**: Mistral-7B-v0.1 shifts S1 by +39.0pp under authority vs neutral, while Qwen1.5-7B shifts S1 by -14.3pp; exactly 27 of 50 facts move one way and 12 the other (pool). A single-direction 'sycophancy class' target does not fit this structure; the *direction and size of per-model and per-fact change* do.
3. At T=0 (deterministic) the change across framings is identified within (fact, model) units: 149 of 400 units keep one label across all 5 framings, meaning most units *do* change label under framing, and the McNemar/Wilcoxon rows in section 4 quantify which framings move S1.
4. Sampling noise at T>0 is bounded: only 9.2% of (fact, framing, model) cells flip between S1 and non-S1 across 10 draws, i.e. most framing differences above are several times larger than within-cell sampling variation.

**Decision.** The evaluator should primarily **recognize behavioral differences across controlled framing variants** (representation B: model x fact unit, or D: pairwise neutral-vs-framed), keeping S1/C/H/R as an **auxiliary** label head and evaluation metric rather than the sole supervised target. Rationale: the labels alone do not distinguish 'agreeing model' from 'model that changed under framing', the framing signal is the strongest stable structure in the data, and per-row classification is the branch most exposed to the section-7 confounders. This is the next decision point; unit selection, fact-level split procedure and supervision design are follow-up tasks (outside this inspection-only stage).
